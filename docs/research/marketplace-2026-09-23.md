# NexMart marketplace research — 23 September 2026

NexMart should make buying and resolving an order dependable before expanding its feature count. The strongest practical direction is a compact, photo-led storefront with useful product information, clear seller and delivery terms, accurate totals, and visible after-sales actions. Existing discovery features can support a distinctive experience once these fundamentals agree with the backend.

This is a research and code-inspection snapshot taken before the accompanying rebuild. Recommendations below are not implementation or security sign-off. Source descriptions establish what competitors document; they do not establish the reliability of those services or guarantee availability for every product, seller, location, or account.

The planned Exa research was blocked: anonymous initialization at `https://mcp.exa.ai/mcp` returned **HTTP 403 Forbidden**, before `tools/list` or any `web_search_exa` call. No Exa searches were made. The skill's suggested next access route is a configured OAuth connection; the cause of this particular 403 was not established. The recommended storefront direction is concrete: a persistent, easily recognized search field; clear delivery location, account, orders, and cart actions; compact department navigation; one principal photo composition; and product/category rows visible early on mobile. Keep the user on a coherent light customer journey through sign-in and checkout. Use compact, legible task layouts for sellers. These are design proposals for NexMart, not claims that all competitors share one visual system.

Product and category imagery should show the actual listed item, its variant, packaging, or relevant use. Prefer seller/manufacturer-authorized photos with consistent framing and responsive sizes. Generated editorial artwork can help a promotional composition, but should not act as evidence of a product's real appearance or contents. Preserve thumbnails, useful specifications, and pricing when image loading fails. Reserve image geometry to avoid layout jumps. Load the main commerce image promptly and defer secondary media. [S8, S11]

Motion should explain an interaction: gallery changes, adding an item, opening a sheet, or changing a filter. Provide a static reduced-motion experience and a clear pause mechanism for any continuing carousel. Do not hide essential buying information behind animation or hover. Measure the result on a 375px viewport, with a keyboard, and under a constrained network; separately measure real-user performance after deployment. [S11; repository accessibility rules]

Several defensible differentiators can be built from the existing data. These are proposed NexMart experiences, **not claims of worldwide novelty or competitor absence**.

| Proposal | Useful customer/seller outcome | Smallest credible implementation and acceptance condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Compare the complete offer** | A customer can compare price, condition, dispatch estimate, return terms, warranty, and seller identity together. | Extend the existing offer/compare UI with `SellerListing` fields. Preserve the chosen seller and SKU; identify seller-stated terms; label unknown delivery costs. Never convert “verified seller” into an unsupported authenticity guarantee. |
| **Build within my budget** | A customer can choose a category or a small basket and see what remains after item prices and known delivery charges. | Extend `BudgetTool` with category filters and an itemized total using the shared price contract. Explain “₹X remaining” only after applicable charges are known; revalidate all price/stock choices before ordering. |
| **Explain why this matches** | A customer sees which chosen requirements a product satisfies and which are unknown. | Use explicit category specifications and selected filters: e.g. capacity, dimensions, or compatibility. Produce deterministic reason chips with links to specifications. Do not invent suitability or use an AI confidence score as product evidence. [S8] |
| **One purchase record** | A customer sees the purchased seller terms, invoice, shipment events, return eligibility, and any support case in one place. | Snapshot the offer/policy version on order creation; use the existing order page as the entry point. Add case and return-request states with attachments, access checks, and an audit trail. Display missing data honestly. [S1, S5, S7] |
| **Seller next-action desk** | A seller knows the next useful action: correct a listing, replenish stock, dispatch an order, or answer a return case. | Derive tasks from actual moderation, stock, fulfillment, and case states. Link every task to the responsible record. State how any due date was calculated; do not fabricate a reputation or trust score. [S2, S4] |
| **Price/stock watch with an explanation** | An opted-in customer knows what changed for the exact variant and seller they saved. | Add saved watches, real price/stock observations, notification preferences, deduplication, and unsubscribe. Start history when observations begin; never manufacture old prices or send alerts that claim stock is reserved. |

Offer negotiation is feasible later, but it is not a small card action. It needs seller opt-in, minimum-price rules, quote expiry, attempt limits, one-time acceptance, stock handling, and checkout integration. `SellerListing.ts` currently has no negotiation contract in the inspected schema. Its value should be tested after baseline conversion and after-sales usability are reliable.

An implementation order follows from the evidence:

1. Correct misleading trust copy and real design/behavior defects; preserve cart/variant identity; complete desktop/mobile/keyboard purchase journeys; resolve the separate authentication/payment/Render audit findings.
2. Deliver customer cancellation/return requests, operator response, refund tracking, and real support/business details. Establish serviceability and policy data instead of hardcoded guarantees.
3. Improve the selected-offer experience, seller task queues, statement clarity, bulk validation, and the first three differentiator prototypes above.
4. Add promotions, watches, richer reviews/Q&A, seller insights, and messaging with their actual backend/operations. Consider rewards, subscriptions, installment finance, open-box delivery, express logistics, and advertising only with the corresponding business and provider capabilities.

Success should be measured through completed tasks: find an appropriate variant; compare two actual offers; understand payable total and return terms; recover a declined/interrupted payment; find an invoice; request eligible help; and complete a seller dispatch. Track failures and time taken alongside conversion. Do not substitute fabricated reviews, inflated savings, universal return badges, or nonfunctional service promises for those outcomes.
